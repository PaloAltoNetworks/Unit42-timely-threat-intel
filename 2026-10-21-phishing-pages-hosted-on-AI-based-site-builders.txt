2026-10-21 (Wednesday): Phishing pages hosted on AI-based site builders

AUTHORS:

- Shawn He Shuang, Keerthiraj Nagaraj

NOTES:

- We've noted numerous phishing pages hosted on AI-based site builders
- These pages almost always have a DOM hash that no other host shares
  -- Therefore, DOM-hash signatures miss them
- Most such pages still carry the builder's own marker in their markup
  -- We can hunt for it together with other common phishing indicators
- Pages hosted on AI-based site builders more often use a modern JavaScript shell
- They also more often contain code that sends form data to popular chat apps

DETAILS:

- Unique DOM hash:
  -- These pages have an exact DOM hash that no other host shares 
     --- This is for almost every phishing page hosted on an AI-based site builder
     --- We found a rate of 1.00 distinct hashes per host on AI-based site builders
     --- Versus a rate of 0.27 on non-AI-based site builders, like pages.dev
  -- The consequences are: 
     --- A hash rule built from one page matches only that page
     --- Copies of one kit look like separate sites
         ---- On one builder, 74% of hosts fall into same-layout groups of 5 or more
         ---- And the largest is 71 hosts of one phishing kit that impersonates banks
     --- Defenders need other signals: 
         ---- A normalized-page hash
         ---- Screenshot similarity
         ---- Or the builder's own marker

- Modern JS framework: Next.js or Vite/React shell: 
  -- 26.2% of pages hosted on AI-based site builders versus 3.2% of pages from non-AI-based builders
  -- Likely reasons:
     --- The builder generates that stack by default
     --- One platform's pages are 99% Next.js
     --- The others load hashed Vite bundles
  -- Component libraries make a polished brand look-alike login quick to produce
  -- A client-rendered page serves a near-empty shell
    --- Scanners that read only raw HTML may find few indicators
    --- This was not tested

- Chat-app sink (Telegram’s 'sendMessage' or Discord webhook in page code):
  -- 29.0% of AI-based builder pages against 15.9% of non-AI-based builder pages
  -- Likely reasons:
     --- A front-end page has no server
     --- So the browser posts the typed credentials straight to the bot
  -- The attacker gets each submission at once in a chat
