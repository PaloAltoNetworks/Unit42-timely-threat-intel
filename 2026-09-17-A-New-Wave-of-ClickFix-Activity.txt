NOTES:

- We identified a new wave of ClickFix attacks compromising over 350 websites linked to a leading CMS platform. 
- Most of these compromises are still active and we encourage security providers to update their IOCs (some of these IOCs have low VT scores at the moment and are likely not picked up by URL blocking services), and YARA signatures to block the ongoing attacks.
The defining characteristic of this campaign is the deliberate abuse of jsDelivr, one of the internet’s most widely trusted open-source CDNs, as a payload staging server, combined with GitHub as the underlying payload store.
- cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/<user>/<repo>@<commit>/<file>. Because cdn.jsdelivr.net is a legitimate CDN that serves millions of open-source libraries daily, the download URL passes almost all domain-reputation filters and corporate proxy allowlists without inspection.

DETAILS:

Attack Chain:

- Stage 1: Lure and clipboard injection: Victims reach one of 350+ compromised sites, where an injected JavaScript bundle renders a fake verification button. 
   - Clicking it silently writes a PowerShell command to the clipboard and instructs the victim to open PowerShell and paste.

- Stage 2: WebDAV PE dropper: The script retrieved from jsDelivr maps a temporary network drive over HTTPS WebDAV to attacker infrastructure, reads a PE binary disguised as a .txt file, and drops it to %LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\Windows\INetCache\<12-char-random>.exe

- Stage 3: Final Payload: A PE binary is served from dedicated WebDAV C2 domains. 
   - The dropper architecture matches techniques associated with infostealer campaigns targeting browser credentials, session cookies, and cryptocurrency wallets.
